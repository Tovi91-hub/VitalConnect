# Compliance Prerequisites

Distributors do not open accounts on enthusiasm. Every item below gets asked for on a
credit application or a reseller agreement, and a missing one stalls the application for
weeks. Assemble the packet once, reuse it for every supplier.

> Not legal advice. Device licensing is state-by-state and changes; confirm the specific
> requirements with a healthcare regulatory attorney before the first regulated sale.

## Part 1 — Account application packet

| Item | Why the supplier wants it | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Registered legal entity (MyVital Harmony LLC) with articles of organization | The agreement is signed by an entity, not a person | ☐ |
| EIN | Credit application and tax reporting | ☐ |
| State resale certificate / sales-tax permit | Lets them sell to you tax-exempt for resale | ☐ |
| Business bank account in the entity's name | Payment terms and credit checks | ☐ |
| Business address (not a residence, where avoidable) | Some distributors will not ship wholesale to residential | ☐ |
| Product liability insurance, typically $1–2M per occurrence, distributor named as additional insured | Standard precondition in reseller agreements | ☐ |
| Trade references (2–3) and/or D-U-N-S number | Credit terms | ☐ |
| Live website showing the categories to be sold | They check that the storefront matches the application | ☐ |
| Written statement of channel: own site only, or also Amazon/Walmart marketplaces | Many brands restrict marketplace resale specifically | ☐ |

The insurance line is the one most often skipped and most often fatal to an application.
Get a quote before applying — for a drop-ship retailer with no manufacturing it is usually
affordable, and having the certificate in hand shortens the process.

## Part 2 — Selling other companies' devices (the retailer position)

Reselling finished, already-labeled devices under the manufacturer's brand is the lighter
regulatory position, but it is not zero:

- **FDA establishment registration** under 21 CFR Part 807 applies to establishments
  involved in the production and distribution of devices. A pure retailer selling
  finished, manufacturer-labeled OTC devices is generally not the registering party — the
  manufacturer and initial importer are. Confirm this per product line rather than
  assuming it across the catalog.
- **State licensing is the live exposure.** States, not FDA, primarily police the
  distribution chain. Most states license distributors of prescription devices, with
  recordkeeping, storage, facility, security, and reporting requirements attached.
  Requirements apply both in the state of establishment **and in each state shipped
  into** — which for a national e-commerce business means the analysis cannot stop at the
  home state. Examples flagged in the state surveys: Minnesota medical device dealer
  registration (~$500/yr), Oregon device distributor registration via the Board of
  Pharmacy, Maryland wholesale prescription drug and device distributor permit, New Jersey
  wholesale drug and device distributor registration with a bond requirement, Washington
  DOH medical device license.
- **Practical consequence for the launch catalog:** starting with **OTC-only** SKUs — the
  blood-pressure monitors, pulse oximeters, first-aid, PPE, mobility, and caregiver
  supplies already in the plan — avoids the prescription-device licensing tier almost
  entirely. Prescription and DME-billable items are a deliberate later phase, entered on
  purpose with licensing in place, not drifted into because a supplier's feed included
  them.

**Action:** when the supplier feed is imported, prescription-only items must be filtered
out at import, not hidden at the template level. This belongs in the eventual catalog
sync rules.

## Part 3 — Private label (the step that changes the regulatory position)

The plan's phase three is "private label selected products." That step converts
MyVital Harmony from retailer into a **relabeler / specification developer**, and the
obligations change materially:

- FDA establishment registration and device listing become the private labeler's own
  responsibility.
- Quality system obligations (21 CFR Part 820, transitioning to QMSR) attach, including
  complaint handling and design/labeling controls.
- Medical Device Reporting — adverse events and malfunctions must be reported.
- UDI labeling and GUDID submission.
- Recall responsibility sits with the brand on the label. That is MyVital Harmony's label.
- Product liability shifts materially toward the brand owner, and insurance must be
  repriced accordingly.

None of that is a reason to abandon private label — it is the highest-margin phase and
worth reaching. It *is* a reason not to slide into it casually because a factory offered
to put a logo on a cuff. Budget for a regulatory consultant before the first private-label
purchase order, and treat it as a funded project phase with its own timeline.

The bundles in the plan (Home Recovery Kit, Senior Home Safety Kit, and so on) sit in a
middle position: assembling a **convenience kit** of finished, individually-labeled
devices under a MyVital Harmony kit name can pull in relabeler/kit-assembler obligations
depending on how the kit is labeled and whether the components' own labeling stays intact.
The conservative launch version — a curated multi-item cart or "shop the kit" collection
where each product ships in its own manufacturer packaging — captures most of the average
order value benefit with none of the labeling exposure. Start there; graduate to a
physically co-packed, MyVital-Harmony-labeled kit only with regulatory review.

## Part 4 — Claims discipline

The marketing plan's weakest legal point is claims. Rules to encode in the content
process from day one:

- Never state or imply that a product diagnoses, treats, cures, or prevents disease
  unless that exact claim is within the device's cleared/approved labeling.
- Do not write "medical grade," "hospital grade," or "FDA approved" as generic
  reassurance. "FDA cleared" and "FDA approved" mean specific things and are checkable.
- Product copy should stay within the manufacturer's own claim language. Copying the
  manufacturer's description is safer than improving on it.
- Kit and bundle names must describe contents or use case, not outcomes. "Post-Surgery
  Home Essentials" is defensible; "Recovery Accelerator" is not.
- Every testimonial or review referencing a health outcome is a claim attributable to the
  seller. Moderate accordingly.
